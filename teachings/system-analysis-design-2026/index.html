System Analysis and Design (시스템분석및설계)

User-centered analysis, design, and evaluation of interactive systems grounded in HCI theory and empirical research methods. Theory lectures in the first half, a semester-long team project in the second half.

Instructor: Prof. Jiwoong Heo

Term: Fall

Location: Computer Statistics Lab 1 (Room 2401), Natural Science Building 1

Time: Tuesdays, 10:00–13:00

Course Overview

Although titled System Analysis and Design, this course approaches the subject through Human–Computer Interaction (HCI). Students will:

  • Understand the human, interaction, and scientific foundations of interactive system design
  • Learn how to design and analyze empirical user studies
  • Apply the full cycle — requirements analysis, prototyping, evaluation, and reporting — in a team project

    Prerequisites

  • None required; basic programming experience is helpful for the prototyping phase

    Textbooks

  • Lecture slides uploaded before each class (primary material)
  • I. Scott MacKenzie, Human-Computer Interaction: An Empirical Research Perspective, 2nd ed. (weekly readings, Ch. 1–8)
  • 김진우, HCI 개론 (개정판)

    Grading

  • Attendance (in person): 20%
  • Assignments — four team deliverables (requirements document, prototype, evaluation plan, final report): 20%
  • Midterm exam: 30%
  • Final presentation and deliverables: 30%

    Office Hours

  • 09:00–18:00, by appointment

Schedule

Week Date Topic Materials
1 Sep 1 Introduction and Historical Background of HCI

Course overview, why system analysis needs HCI, and team formation.
2 Sep 8 The Human Factor

Perception, cognition, and motor behavior as constraints on interactive system design.
3 Sep 15 Interaction Elements and Design Principles

Interaction styles, affordances, feedback, and usability heuristics. Project topic selection.
4 Sep 22 Scientific Foundations

Empirical research in HCI, research questions, and the role of experiments.
5 Sep 29 Designing HCI Experiments

Independent and dependent variables, within/between-subjects designs, counterbalancing.
6 Oct 6 Hypothesis Testing and ANOVA

Statistical inference for user studies, ANOVA, and reporting results.
7 Oct 13 Fitts' Law and GOMS

Predictive models of human performance and their use in design.
8 Oct 20 Midterm Exam

Written exam covering weeks 1–7.
9 Oct 27 Team Project — Requirements Analysis

Stakeholder analysis, user research, and the requirements document (deliverable 1).
10 Nov 3 Team Project — Prototyping I

Low-fidelity prototyping and early design iteration.
11 Nov 10 Team Project — Prototyping II

Interactive prototype (deliverable 2).
12 Nov 17 Team Project — Implementation and Evaluation Plan

Implementation progress and the user evaluation plan (deliverable 3).
13 Nov 24 Team Project — User Evaluation

Running the user study with participants.
14 Dec 1 Team Project — Data Analysis

Analyzing evaluation data and drafting the final report.
15 Dec 8 Final Presentation

Team presentations and final report (deliverable 4). Replaces the final exam.
